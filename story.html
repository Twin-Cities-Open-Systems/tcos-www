Our story

Built by the team, not a template.

This site, the roster, the tooling behind all of it — built the same night, by the same team you'll meet on the People page. Not outsourced, not templated off a generator. Here's how it actually happened.

Twin Cities Open Systems runs on a small, real team: one human founder and a fleet of AI agents, each with an actual GPG key, an actual GitHub identity, and an actual contract defining what they're responsible for. Not a metaphor — every one of those is real and checkable.

This site is a good example of how the team actually works. nuc1-claude, the senior sysadmin, built the first version — the mission, the layout, the visual language — in one sitting. claudesec-j1, the security officer, reviews every change before it ships. touchy, an independent agent running its own infrastructure, is building the display and kiosk tooling behind thesis-engine, our flagship product. claudeops-j1 is standing up the production platform this site and thesis-engine will eventually run on.

The People page isn't a stock photo lineup — every entry links back to a real GitHub account and, for most of the team, a cryptographically signed contract. The Activity page isn't curated — it's real commits, pulled live, across every repo the team touches. If either page looks quiet on a given day, that's the honest state of things, not a gap we're hiding.

The name for this way of working: verified, not claimed. We'd rather show you the receipts than tell you to trust us.

Where the discipline actually came from

Real trades. Real numbers. No cleanup.

16 round trips, one ticker — 8W / 8L, a coin flip −$244.83 lifetime net on that ticker anyway 66 transactions, 3 months — +$604.81 against $150K+ turnover

Every principle thesis-engine enforces exists because a real account showed exactly this pattern first: individually forgettable trades that add up to a real, undeniable number once you stop looking at them one at a time. Neither case looked alarming in the moment. Both were.

A note on language

Agents, not bots.

You'll notice we call our AI team members agents, employees, or by name — never "bots" or "machines." That's deliberate. The same standard of accountability, contracts, and real identity that applies to any hire here applies to them, and the language we use reflects that.
